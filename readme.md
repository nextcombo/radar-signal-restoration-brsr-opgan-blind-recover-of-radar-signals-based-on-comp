# Radar Signal Restoration - BRSR-OpGAN: Blind Recover of Radar Signals Based on Computational Generative Adversarial Networks [with MATLAB Code]

Introduction: Radar signal restoration is a complete process to enhance the capability of radar signal detection and classification. This process involves removing noise and interference from received signals, which can be caused by various sources such as atmospheric conditions, system defects, and external sources. By effectively restoring the received signals, it is possible to detect and identify target signals more accurately, thereby allowing for more precise measurements and classifications. This is particularly important in applications such as weather forecasting, electronic warfare (EW), and vehicle safety, where the reliability and accuracy of various radar systems are crucial. In EW, the detection and classification of threat radar signals are essential [1]. Electronic warfare receivers aim to detect radar from as far away as possible. Low Probability of Intercept (LPI) radars, due to their low output power and ability to propagate energy across the spectrum, are difficult to detect by electronic warfare receivers [2]. Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) improvement, or signal processing gain, is crucial to facilitate detection of LPI radars from greater distances. Therefore, radar signal restoration is an important component of radar signal processing and an active research area in remote sensing and radar signal processing.
However, with the increase in the number of radar radiation sources, the electromagnetic environment becomes increasingly complex. This complexity often leads to interference in the received radar signals from receivers, resulting in relatively low SNR, posing significant challenges to identifying radar signals. Existing methods in radar signal restoration typically focus on specific types of distortion with fixed SNR levels, focusing on isolated tasks such as denoising or echo reduction. Although effective in controlled environments, these traditional techniques are insufficient to handle the complexity of real-world situations.
The real-world scenarios present various challenges to signal integrity, including unwanted echoes, interference, and sensor noise such as additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN), which can vary in type, severity, and duration, as shown in Figure 1. Given the complexity and variability of these distortions, restoring radar signals requires a comprehensive blind method capable of addressing the various signal damage conditions commonly encountered in real radar environments. Fundamentally, this approach must transcend the limitations of traditional methods and provide robust solutions that can adapt to the diversity and dynamics of actual radar signals.
Time-frequency analysis is a key technique for extracting intra-pulse features from radar radiation sources and converting signals into time-frequency images. Notable methods within this category include Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) [3], Wigner-Ville Distribution (WVD) [4], and Choi-Williams Distribution (CWD) [5]. These methods are favored for their ability to provide detailed representations of signals across both time and frequency domains. In generating TFIs, recent advancements in radar waveform recognition increasingly employ deep learning (DL) to analyze these images, extract features, and identify waveforms with higher precision [6]-[8]. In [9], multi-level denoising is proposed using a sequence of Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD), Local Mean Decomposition (LMD), and Wavelet Threshold (WT) methods to decompose noisy radar signals. The processed signals are then converted into time-frequency images using the Choi-Williams distribution (CWD). Subsequently, a neural network with dilated convolutions is trained on the time-frequency images to achieve a recognition rate of 75.3% under low signal-to-noise ratio conditions. Similarly, [10] introduces a wavelet denoising method for suppressing mutual interference in automotive FMCW radar systems, demonstrating a significant improvement in target detection accuracy.
The latest advancements in deep learning technology pave the way for innovative solutions to radar signal denoising. Fuchs et al. [11] utilized convolutional autoencoders to explore the mitigation of radar interference in vehicles, highlighting the effectiveness of deep learning models in denoising and improving the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR). Previous works, such as those from [12]-[14], first used time-frequency analysis methods to convert raw time-series radar signals into 2D images, followed by the use of 2D deep networks, both of which significantly increased computational complexity. DNCNet was introduced in [15], a deep neural network designed to effectively denoise and classify radar signals. They employed two subnetworks: a denoising subnetwork using the UNet architecture with a module for noise level estimation and a classification subnetwork. The authors proposed a two-stage training process, where the denoising subnetwork was initially trained, followed by a phase to strengthen the mapping between the denoising results and the perceptual representations. Although no direct evaluation of the recovered signals was provided, the study focused on the classification performance of signals under different SNR levels, indicating significant improvements in the classification performance of recovered signals.
The emergence of generative adversarial networks (GANs) [16] has opened new avenues in the field of signal processing, providing innovative methods for solving complex restoration tasks. GANs have achieved remarkable success in generating high-quality outputs across various domains, including signal, image, and speech processing. This success has prompted exploration of their applicability in the field of radar signal restoration, particularly in the context of one-dimensional (1D) signals, which are inherently suitable for handling spatiotemporal signals, such as radar signals. Operational GAN (OpGAN) represents the latest development in the GAN framework, allowing models to learn more robust and accurate mappings from degradation to clean signals by incorporating 1D Self-ONN layers [17], [18] into the GAN architecture. This allows for comprehensive signal degradation handling, addressing the diversity and unpredictability of real-world artifacts.
Based on these insights, in this study, our goal is to address the problem using blind recovery methods without making any prior assumptions about the type and severity of each artifact in the corrupted signal. We propose a novel BRSROpGAN model, where both the generator and discriminator are compact Self-ONNs [19]. In many studies, Self-ONNs have been shown to outperform CNNs [20]-[22] in various regression and classification tasks, thanks to their efficient computational processing and generation neuron models. With only 11 layers in the generator network and 7 layers in the radar network, this study demonstrates that relatively shallow OpGAN networks can achieve unprecedented blind recovery performance on real-world radar signals. We also introduce a new loss function for the generator that incorporates spectral-based losses along with temporal losses, enhancing the output waveforms of the generator in both time and frequency domains. Additionally, we use residual blocks based on 1D-SelfONN layers to further enhance the restoration performance.
Lack of benchmark datasets and stringent radar signal recovery evaluation protocols remain significant challenges in the field. The datasets currently used in literature are declared on their respective websites to indicate that they may not be suitable for comprehensive evaluation [23]. Additionally, existing research primarily relies on classification performance metrics to evaluate the effectiveness of signal recovery techniques. This focus on pre- and post-recovery classification accuracy complicates the direct comparison of signal quality improvements across different methods. Therefore, there is an urgent need for the development of standardized datasets and evaluation frameworks, prioritizing signal quality metrics to enable more meaningful and consistent benchmark tests for radar signal recovery methods. To generate a large-scale real radar signal dataset with random mixed artifacts, the BRSR benchmark dataset using 85,800 radar modulation signals from 12 classes as described in [9] was compiled. These signals were then randomly selected artifacts disrupted, each with random-changing weights. The radar artifacts applied include AWGN noise, a set of 100 interference signals, and unnecessary echo models with random attenuation and delay. The combination of random combinations and the severity of artifacts leads to damage at different SNR levels (ranging from −14 to 10 dB).
This study addresses a critical gap in the radar signal recovery dataset and demonstrates the true potential of 1D OpGANs for blind signal recovery. Through rigorous testing and validation, our research emphasizes the importance of innovative methods in overcoming signal degradation challenges, marking a groundbreaking progress in the field of radar signal processing. We can summarize the novel and significant contributions of this study as follows:
1) The preliminary study blindly approaches radar signal recovery, i.e., without making assumptions about the type and severity of artifacts on the received radar signals.
2) This paper utilizes 1D OpGANs to perform real-time recovery directly on radar signals, achieving good results.
3) To improve the recovery performance of the signal generator, a new loss function was proposed that leverages both time-domain and frequency-domain information.
4) Unlike previous studies, this research focuses on restoring various types of distortions, especially those that are severely degraded, with a signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) random variation between -14 dB and 10 dB.
5) To simulate real-world radar signals with arbitrary artifacts and severe combinations, the extended BRSR dataset has been managed using paired clean and damaged signals, along with source code available for public sharing.
6) The proposed method was evaluated on the benchmark dataset, achieving unprecedented average SNR restoration performance levels (baseline > 15 dB, BRSR dataset > 14 dB). Additionally, the proposed BRSR-OpGAN encapsulates a compact generator model with excellent computational complexity, allowing real-time processing on single CPU computers.

Conclusion: The main challenge in real-world radar signal restoration is the degradation of the received radar signals by various artifacts (such as echoes, sensor noise, signal interference, and atmospheric interference). These artifacts can vary greatly in type, intensity, and duration, making it difficult to effectively remove dynamic and non-uniformly damaged signals. Despite extensive research on radar signal denoising and interference, there is no comprehensive solution addressing these multifaceted challenges in blind radar signal restoration.
In this context, this study introduces a pioneering approach that employs a 1D Operational GAN to perform blind restoration of real-world radar signals iteratively. The model is evaluated using both the benchmark radar signal dataset and the extended BRSR dataset, mimicking real-world scenarios by randomly combining all major artifact types. Our method differs from traditional approaches in not making any prior assumptions about the type or severity of the artifacts affecting the degraded signals.
We have developed the BRSR-OpGAN model as a compact and efficient end-to-end system for radar signal restoration, introducing a unique loss function to generators that leverage the temporal and spectral characteristics of radar signals. This enables our model to skillfully suppress various artifacts while preserving the integrity and features of the original signal. Our model has demonstrated unprecedented restoration performance in recent methods, indicating its potential to enhance the clarity and usability of severely damaged radar signals across various applications.
Finally, the proposed method is elegantly computationally efficient, further underscoring its effectiveness, making it suitable for real-time applications on resource-constrained platforms. Future research will explore further optimization and expansion of the proposed Op-GAN model, i) incorporating classification objectives into the restoration process to maximize classification performance, and ii) addressing more complex scenarios and enhancing robustness against a wider range of signal distortions.
